# Weekly Planner

The weekly planner gives you a view of scheduled activities across the week. It helps you see what is coming up, recognize busy periods, and make a more realistic plan for your time.

Use it as a guide for the week, not as a demand to account for every minute.

## Review the week

Start by looking across the week as a whole.

Notice fixed commitments, approaching deadlines, and days that already have a lot scheduled. Seeing several days together can make it easier to identify conflicts and decide where additional work might fit.

## Plan your schedule

When adding or arranging an activity, use a clear title and select the relevant date and time when those fields are available.

As you plan:

1. **Start with fixed commitments.** Account for activities that already have a set time.
2. **Add important responsibilities.** Consider deadlines and other work that needs dedicated time.
3. **Choose realistic work sessions.** Break larger responsibilities into manageable periods.
4. **Leave room to adjust.** Allow for transitions, breaks, and unexpected changes.

A schedule is more useful when it reflects the time you actually have available.

## Connect tasks with your schedule

Tasks and scheduled activities play different roles.

- A **task** represents work that needs to be completed.
- A **scheduled activity** represents when you intend to do something.

For example, *Finish science notes* can be a task, while *Review science notes on Tuesday afternoon* describes when you plan to work on it.

Use categories, where available, to keep different areas of your schedule organized.

## Keep the week balanced

A good weekly plan accounts for more than deadlines.

- Avoid filling every available period.
- Break demanding work into smaller sessions.
- Leave time between activities when needed.
- Consider which responsibilities require the most attention.
- Keep some flexibility for changes.

Planning everything too tightly can make a schedule difficult to follow. Leave enough room for the week to unfold.

## Review and adjust

Your schedule will not always go exactly as planned. When something changes, review the remaining commitments and adjust accordingly.

You might move a work session, reconsider a priority, or leave a less urgent task for another day. The goal is to maintain a useful overview, not to preserve the original plan at all costs.

## Make weekly planning a habit

A brief review at the beginning of the week can help you establish priorities. A quick check during the week can help you adapt as circumstances change.

Keep the routine simple enough to repeat. The planner should reduce uncertainty and help you decide what to do next.
